I've been on a journey these past few months. One of soft leadership and nuanced direction setting. In my recent time as a Technical Lead I've learned a lot of things that I honestly wish I didn't in true "ignorance is bliss" style.

I'm an engineer by trade but my academic background is Computer Science. I did do 2 years of Computer Engineering before I decided that anything hardware related is not my cup of tea. I was simply taken by Data Structures and Algorithms from the beginning. The first class had me hooked and hasn't let me go since then! I've been struck by how much of my learnings from this can be applied to my very Finance orientated engineering career, specifically the way information flows in an organisation.





1. Information flows across, down and up a functional hierarchy - but different pieces are lost depending on which direction is goes
2. Each node in the hierarchy has a certain level of entropy
3. Information (split by knowledge type) has different levels of density depending on where you are in the organisation

Areas of the organisation:

I'm going to intentionally ignore and exclude the following:
1. Incentive structures - I'll consider this a part of the people strategy of the organisation folded into the side structures (seen below)
2. Personal motivations - I'll let the "entropy of the individual" cater for this

Top:

This is classical and the most commonly understood and advertised. Most of the knowledge types that flow this way are strategy dense. I like this definition of strategy:

> An integrative set of choices that positions you on a playing field of your choice in a way that you win. - Roger Martin

So the top is doing 3 things:
1. Making choices
2. Deciding what the playing field is
3. Trying to win

The flow of information down is thusly orientated towards achieving that.

Down:

The "foot-soldiers" of the organisation - individuals - are the ones that are executing and implementing the units of work to get to a goal. In other words, they functionally contribute to the strategy directly. Note that this is influenced by lots of things, some of them being:

1. The functional role of the worker
2. The culture of the team/squad/grouping the worker is in
4. The direction the individual's team is set on going towards

There is obviously a lot more complexity and nuance then these 4 broad points but sufficient enough for the purposes of discussing this topic in my opinion.

Sides:

This is the least understood area of the org. This would be non-functional but necessary areas where the propose of the area is to serve the structure directly rather then to serve the strategy directly. These areas are very nuanced.


Flow of information:

1. Top - down

Communication flows with low levels of, but highly precise information. It's up to the subsequent level down to bulk out this communication with implementation details. So information becomes more dense when flowing downwards.

Of course - information doesn't come from nowhere. Looking at the high-level overview of "Down" - this new information will be varied. The level of variance will rely on cross-entropy information distribution - what I mean here is that moving across different levels of entropy will cause variance and unreliability proportional to the differences in source and destination.

An immediate and obvious conclusion to draw here is that information flowing down is going to be more accurate the more aligned the individuals are. In our case, alignment about where we need to go and what the strategy is would probably be the most important factor.

2. Down - up

The flow here is one of compression. This is an area that is more comfortable to talk about because compression is a known beast in computer science.
